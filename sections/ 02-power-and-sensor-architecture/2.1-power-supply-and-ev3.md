Cheese uses a centralized power system. The **EV3 rechargeable battery** powers the EV3 Brick, and the EV3 Brick distributes power to the motors, sensors, Arduino Nano, and HuskyLens system. The only exception is the auxiliary lighting system, which uses a separate 9V battery to support stable illumination for vision and color detection.

For a beginner, the power system can be understood like this:

<div align="center">

| Power Element | Simple Explanation | Main Job |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EV3 rechargeable battery** | The main energy source of the robot. | Powers the EV3 Brick and most electronic components. |
| **EV3 Brick** | The control center and power distributor. | Sends power and commands to motors and sensors. |
| **Motor ports** | Output ports for motors. | Power the drive motor and steering motor. |
| **Sensor ports** | Input ports for sensors. | Power and read the ultrasonic and color sensors. |
| **EV3 USB port** | USB power and communication port. | Powers the Arduino Nano. |
| **Arduino Nano** | Interface between EV3 and HuskyLens. | Powers and communicates with the HuskyLens. |
| **Auxiliary 9V battery** | Separate power source for lamps. | Keeps lighting independent from the EV3 battery. |

</div>

Stable power matters because Cheese’s motors, sensors, camera, and control logic can only behave consistently when the battery and wiring system provide reliable voltage during testing.

## ❀ 2.1.1 The Main Power Source ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Battery-EV3_Rechargeable_Li--ion-FFD43B?style=for-the-badge">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Nominal_Voltage-7.4V-57C785?style=for-the-badge">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Capacity-2050mAh-4A90E2?style=for-the-badge">

The main power source of Cheese is the **LEGO EV3 rechargeable Li-ion battery pack**. This battery powers the EV3 Brick, and the EV3 Brick then powers the motors, sensors, and USB-connected Arduino Nano.

<div align="center">

| Item | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Main battery** | LEGO EV3 rechargeable Li-ion battery pack |
| **Capacity** | 2050 mAh |
| **Nominal voltage** | ~7.4V |
| **Battery rotation** | 2 packs used in rotation |
| **Main powered systems** | EV3 Brick, EV3 Large Motor, EV3 Medium Motor, ultrasonic sensors, color sensor, Arduino Nano, HuskyLens |
| **Separate auxiliary power** | 9V battery for the two helping lamps |

</div>

We use two EV3 battery packs in rotation. While one battery is on the robot, the other can be charging. This helps us test with more consistent voltage instead of trying to tune the robot with a half-drained battery.

This is important because a run on a weak battery is not the same as a run on a fresh battery. The robot may still turn on, but the motors and camera system may behave differently.

## ❀ 2.1.2 How Power Is Distributed ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese uses a centralized power architecture. The EV3 Brick acts as the main distribution point. The motors connect to EV3 output ports, the sensors connect to EV3 input ports, and the Arduino Nano receives power through the EV3 USB port.

The HuskyLens camera is powered through the Arduino Nano. This means the vision system depends on the EV3 battery indirectly: EV3 battery → EV3 Brick → USB → Arduino Nano → HuskyLens.

<div align="center">

| Component | Port / Connection | Powered By | Approx. Voltage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EV3 Large Motor** | `OUTPUT_B` | EV3 battery through EV3 motor output | ~7.4V nominal motor supply |
| **EV3 Medium Motor** | `OUTPUT_A` | EV3 battery through EV3 motor output | ~7.4V nominal motor supply |
| **Ultrasonic sensor left** | `INPUT_1` | EV3 sensor port | ~5V logic/sensor supply |
| **Color sensor** | `INPUT_2` | EV3 sensor port | ~5V logic/sensor supply |
| **Ultrasonic sensor right** | `INPUT_3` | EV3 sensor port | ~5V logic/sensor supply |
| **Arduino Nano** | EV3 USB | EV3 USB power | 5V |
| **HuskyLens camera** | I2C through Arduino Nano | Arduino Nano 5V and GND pins | 5V |
| **Helping lamps** | Separate wiring | External 9V battery | 9V battery supply |

</div>

The lamps are separate from the EV3 power system. This is useful because the lights support camera and color detection, but they do not drain the EV3 battery directly. The upper lamp helps illuminate the obstacle detection area for the HuskyLens, while the lower lamp helps the color sensor read the floor more consistently.

## ❀ 2.1.3 EV3 Brick as the Power and Control Center ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

## ❀ 2.1.4 Arduino Nano and HuskyLens Power Path ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The Arduino Nano and HuskyLens are part of the vision system. The EV3 Brick does not connect directly to the HuskyLens in the same way it connects to LEGO sensors. Instead, the Arduino Nano works as an intermediate board.

The power path is:

<p align="center">
  <strong>EV3 Battery → EV3 Brick → USB Port → Arduino Nano → HuskyLens</strong>
</p>

This makes the vision system more sensitive to power quality because it depends on several connections. If the EV3 battery becomes weak, or if a cable is loose, the camera system can become less reliable.

<div align="center">

| Vision Power Part | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **EV3 USB port** | Provides 5V power to the Arduino Nano. |
| **Arduino Nano** | Receives USB power and communicates with the HuskyLens. |
| **HuskyLens** | Uses 5V and GND from the Arduino Nano for camera operation. |
| **I2C connection** | Allows the Arduino and HuskyLens to communicate. |

</div>

## ❀ 2.1.5 Auxiliary Lighting Power ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The helping lamps use a separate 9V battery. This is different from the EV3-powered systems. The reason is that the lamps are used for illumination support, not for robot control.

The lights help the robot because both the camera and the color sensor depend on what they can see. If the lighting changes too much, the robot may misread obstacles or floor colors.

<div align="center">

| Lamp | Purpose | Why It Helps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upper lamp** | Illuminates the obstacle detection area. | Helps the HuskyLens see obstacles more clearly. |
| **Lower lamp** | Illuminates the floor/color sensor area. | Helps the color sensor detect floor markers more consistently. |
| **Separate 9V battery** | Powers the lamps independently. | Keeps lighting from draining the EV3 battery directly. |

</div>

This separation makes the power system easier to understand:

<p align="center">
  <strong>EV3 battery = robot control, motors, sensors, and camera system</strong><br>
  <strong>9V battery = auxiliary lighting support</strong>

## ❀ 2.1.6 Why Voltage Matters More Than Battery Percentage ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

A battery does not behave exactly the same from full charge to low charge. Even if the EV3 Brick is still on, the robot may behave differently as voltage drops.

For Cheese, voltage matters because motors depend on electrical power to move. When voltage decreases, motor behavior can change. This affects both the drive motor and the steering motor.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Important-Its_not_only_battery_percent-FF8FAB?style=for-the-badge">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Voltage_Affects-Motor_Response-FFD43B?style=for-the-badge">
</p>

In simple terms:

<div align="center">

| Battery Condition | Possible Robot Behavior |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fresh battery** | Motors respond faster and more consistently. |
| **Partially drained battery** | Motors may feel slower or less sharp. |
| **Weak battery** | Steering may react late, and the camera/Arduino system may become less reliable. |
| **Inconsistent battery level during testing** | Tuning becomes harder because the robot is not behaving the same way each run. |

</div>

This is why we rotate battery packs. It helps keep testing conditions more consistent.

---

## ❀ 2.1.7 Voltage Effect on Motors ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The motors are the most noticeable components affected by battery condition. If the battery voltage drops, the robot can still run, but motor response can become weaker or slower.

For a DC motor, speed is related to supply voltage:

<p align="center">
  <strong>No-load speed ∝ supply voltage</strong>
</p>

This means that if the available voltage drops, the motor’s free-running speed also tends to drop. The robot may still move, but it may not move with the same response as it did with a fresh battery.

The EV3 Large Motor is responsible for drive, and the EV3 Medium Motor is responsible for steering. Both matter, but the steering motor is more sensitive during curves because it must reach the commanded angle before the robot arrives at the wall.

<div align="center">

| Motor | What Low Voltage Can Affect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **EV3 Large Motor** | Forward speed, acceleration, and consistency during long runs. |
| **EV3 Medium Motor** | Steering speed, steering timing, and curve reliability. |

</div>

This connects directly to Section 1.3. If Cheese drives into a curve and the steering motor is slower than expected, the wheels may not reach the correct angle in time.

## ❀ 2.1.8 What Fails First: Steering Timing and Vision Reliability ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

As the battery drains, Cheese may begin to behave differently. The most visible problem is usually not that the robot suddenly turns off. Instead, the robot may become less consistent.

Two systems are especially important:

<div align="center">

| System | Why It Is Sensitive |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Steering system** | It depends on the Medium Motor reaching the commanded angle quickly enough during curves. |
| **Vision system** | The HuskyLens depends on the EV3 USB → Arduino Nano → HuskyLens power path. |

</div>

### Steering timing

If the Medium Motor becomes slower, Cheese may enter a curve before the wheels reach the required angle. This can make the robot turn too late and crash into a wall.

This does not always look like a battery problem at first. It can look like bad code, bad PID tuning, or incorrect steering angle. However, if the same code works better with a fresh battery, then power consistency is part of the problem.

### Vision reliability

The HuskyLens is powered indirectly through the Arduino Nano. This means the camera system depends on the EV3 USB power path and the Nano’s 5V/GND connection. If power becomes unstable, the vision system can become less reliable.

This is why wiring and power routing matter. The camera is not only a software component; it also depends on stable electrical power.

---

## ❀ 2.1.9 Battery Rotation and Testing Consistency ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

We use two EV3 battery packs in rotation because testing with inconsistent battery levels can give misleading results.

A robot may fail a curve for two different reasons:

<div align="center">

| Possible Cause | What It Looks Like |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanical/tuning issue** | The robot fails even with a fresh battery. |
| **Battery voltage issue** | The robot works better with a fresh battery but gets worse as voltage drops. |

</div>

Battery rotation helps separate these problems. When tests are done with a fresh or consistent battery, it becomes easier to know whether a failure comes from mechanical design, software tuning, sensor placement, or power level.

<p align="center">
  <strong>Testing rule:</strong><br>
  Do not compare tuning results from a fresh battery and a weak battery as if they were the same condition.
</p>

## ✦ Final Power Supply Summary ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ───

Cheese’s main power system is centered around the EV3 rechargeable battery. The EV3 Brick powers the drive motor, steering motor, LEGO sensors, and the Arduino Nano through USB. The Arduino Nano then powers and communicates with the HuskyLens camera. The auxiliary lamps use a separate 9V battery so lighting support does not directly drain the EV3 battery.

The most important lesson from this section is that power affects behavior. A weak battery can make motors slower, steering less responsive, and the camera system less reliable. Because of that, battery rotation and wiring organization are part of the robot’s testing strategy, not just maintenance.

<p align="center">
  <strong>Final power lesson:</strong><br>
  Stable power matters because Cheese’s motors, sensors, camera, and control logic can only behave consistently when the EV3 battery and wiring system provide reliable voltage during testing.
</p>

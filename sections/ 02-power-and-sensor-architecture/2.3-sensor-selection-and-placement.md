## ❀ 2.3.1 Sensor Architecture ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese v4 uses multiple sensor types because no single sensor can provide all the information required for autonomous navigation.

The current sensing architecture is divided into three main layers:

```text
DISTANCE SENSING
     ↓
3 × EV3 Ultrasonic Sensors
Left + Right + Front

FLOOR SENSING
     ↓
EV3 Color Sensor

VISION
     ↓
HuskyLens
```

Each layer has a different responsibility.

- The **left and right ultrasonic sensors** provide lateral wall-distance information.
- The **front ultrasonic sensor** provides forward safety information.
- The **color sensor** reads the colored track markings used by the curve-counting system.
- The **HuskyLens** identifies red and green pillars during the Obstacle Challenge.

This separation became important during development because it gives each sensor a specific purpose instead of trying to make one sensor solve every navigation problem.

---

## ❀ 2.3.2 Current Sensor Configuration ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The current Cheese v4 sensor configuration is:

<div align="center">

| Sensor | Quantity | Position | Main Function |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **EV3 Ultrasonic Sensor** | 1 | Left side | Left wall-distance information |
| **EV3 Ultrasonic Sensor** | 1 | Right side | Right wall-distance information |
| **EV3 Ultrasonic Sensor** | 1 | Front | Forward safety and proximity |
| **EV3 Color Sensor** | 1 | Low front / floor-facing | Track color and curve-count information |
| **HuskyLens** | 1 | Front, above the forward ultrasonic | Red/green pillar recognition |

</div>

The physical location of every sensor was treated as part of the sensing system itself.

A sensor may work correctly electronically while still producing poor navigation information if:

- it points at the wrong part of the field,
- it is mounted too high or too low,
- part of the robot blocks its view,
- vibration changes its orientation,
- or the robot approaches the object from an angle the sensor cannot observe reliably.

For this reason, **sensor placement and software calibration were developed together**.

## ❀ 2.3.3 Overall Sensor Placement ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/side_sensor_layout_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 side sensor layout"
       width="78%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Side sensor layout of Cheese v4 showing how the sensing components are integrated into the chassis.</em>
</p>

The current sensor layout attempts to keep the sensing components relatively low and close to the areas they need to observe.

The two lateral ultrasonic sensors are positioned to observe the side walls.

The front ultrasonic sensor faces the direction of travel.

The color sensor faces downward toward the field.

The HuskyLens is mounted toward the front so that colored pillars can enter its field of view before Cheese reaches them.

This creates a sensing architecture where different physical directions correspond to different navigation questions:

```text
LEFT
  ↓
How close is the left wall?

RIGHT
  ↓
How close is the right wall?

FRONT
  ↓
Is Cheese becoming dangerously close to something ahead?

FLOOR
  ↓
Which track color is Cheese crossing?

CAMERA
  ↓
Is the visible pillar red or green?
```

# 2.3.4 Lateral Ultrasonic Sensors

The left and right ultrasonic sensors are the primary distance sensors used for lateral navigation.

Unlike the front ultrasonic sensor, their main role is not emergency stopping.

Instead, they provide information about Cheese's position relative to the walls.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/sensor_placement_left_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 left ultrasonic sensor placement"
       width="46%">
  &nbsp;
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/sensor_placement_right_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 right ultrasonic sensor placement"
       width="46%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Left and right ultrasonic sensor placement on Cheese v4.</em>
</p>

The lateral sensors are mounted so they can continuously measure the surrounding walls while the robot moves through the track.

Their information is used by the navigation software to determine whether Cheese is:

- drifting toward one side,
- moving approximately centered,
- approaching a wall,
- or reaching a distance where stronger wall protection is necessary.

---

## ❀ 2.3.5 Why Two Lateral Sensors? ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Using sensors on both sides provides Cheese with information about both boundaries of the track.

Conceptually:

```text
LEFT WALL       CHEESE       RIGHT WALL
    │              │              │
    │ ←── UL ──── [🧀] ──── UR ─→ │
    │              │              │
```

Where:

```text
UL = Left Ultrasonic Distance
UR = Right Ultrasonic Distance
```

The software can compare these measurements and determine which steering correction is appropriate.

This is especially useful during normal Open Challenge movement because Cheese does not need to follow a completely pre-programmed geometric trajectory.

Instead, the robot can react to its measured position.

---

## ❀ 2.3.6 Why the Lateral Sensors Are Mounted Low ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The current lateral sensors are positioned relatively low on the chassis.

This placement was chosen so their measurement direction corresponds more closely with the field walls rather than unnecessary upper structures or objects outside the useful navigation area.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/side_sensor_layout_v4.jpg"
       alt="Low lateral ultrasonic sensor layout on Cheese v4"
       width="72%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>The low lateral sensor arrangement keeps the ultrasonic sensing direction focused on the track boundaries.</em>
</p>

A consistent physical orientation is important because the software assumes the measurements represent meaningful wall distances.

If the sensor angle changes, the same physical robot position can produce a different measurement.

Therefore, sensor mounting is not only a structural decision.

It is part of the calibration of the navigation system.

---

# ❀ 2.3.7 Front Ultrasonic Sensor ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The third ultrasonic sensor faces forward.

Its role is different from the lateral sensors.

Instead of primarily measuring lateral position, it provides **forward safety information**.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/sensor_placement_front_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 front ultrasonic sensor placement"
       width="70%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Current placement of the forward ultrasonic sensor used for front-distance safety and recovery.</em>
</p>


The front ultrasonic sensor is useful when Cheese approaches the front wall too closely.

This allows the navigation system to recognize dangerous proximity and activate recovery behavior before the robot remains pressed against the wall.

<div align="center">

| Front Sensor Role | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Forward distance** | Detects how much space remains ahead |
| **Safety confirmation** | Helps confirm that the robot is dangerously close |
| **Reverse recovery support** | Provides information used during front-wall recovery |
| **Collision prevention** | Adds a safety layer beyond lateral PID correction |

</div>

---

## ❀ 2.3.8 Why Front Distance Needs a Separate Sensor ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The side ultrasonic sensors cannot fully describe what is happening directly in front of Cheese.

For example, Cheese may enter a curve or leave a correction at an angle where:

```text
LEFT DISTANCE  = acceptable
RIGHT DISTANCE = acceptable

BUT

FRONT DISTANCE = dangerous
```

In this situation, relying only on the lateral sensors could allow the robot to continue toward the wall.

The front ultrasonic sensor provides an independent measurement for this case.

The software can therefore distinguish between:

```text
LATERAL POSITION PROBLEM
        ↓
PID / WALL CORRECTION
```

and:

```text
FRONT PROXIMITY PROBLEM
        ↓
SAFETY / REVERSE RECOVERY
```

This division of responsibilities makes the sensor architecture easier to understand and debug.

---

# ❀ 2.3.9 Color Sensor Selection ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese uses an EV3 Color Sensor to detect the colored markings on the competition field.

Unlike the ultrasonic sensors, the color sensor does not measure wall distance.

Its main role is to provide **track-event information**.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/color_sensor_front_layout_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 color sensor front layout"
       width="68%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Current Cheese v4 color sensor position near the front and close to the field surface.</em>
</p>

The current Open Challenge software uses the color sensor as part of the curve-counting system.

Rather than estimating the three laps only from elapsed time, Cheese can use physical markers on the field to track its progress.

This provides a direct connection between:

```text
PHYSICAL FIELD
      ↓
COLOR SENSOR
      ↓
COLOR VALIDATION
      ↓
CURVE COUNT
      ↓
RUN PROGRESS
      ↓
FINAL PARKING
```

---

## ❀ 2.3.10 Color Sensor Height ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The distance between the color sensor and the field is especially important.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/color_sensor_floor_gap_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 color sensor floor gap"
       width="68%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Close-up showing the current color sensor height relative to the track surface.</em>
</p>

During earlier development, environmental light affected color detection.

A temporary physical cover was tested around the sensor to reduce external light.

However, that solution introduced another problem: a structure mounted too low could interfere physically with the field.

For v4, the color sensor remained in the same general structural region but was positioned **slightly lower**, improving its relationship with the floor.

This allowed the team to improve detection without keeping the previous temporary light-blocking structure as part of normal operation.

The development process therefore became:

```text
LIGHTING AFFECTS COLOR READING
          ↓
TRY PHYSICAL LIGHT BLOCKING
          ↓
PHYSICAL INTERFERENCE APPEARS
          ↓
RECONSIDER SENSOR GEOMETRY
          ↓
LOWER SENSOR SLIGHTLY
          ↓
IMPROVED FLOOR DETECTION
```

This was an important lesson because the final solution was simpler than adding more hardware.

---

# ❀ 2.3.11 Color Sensor Role in Curve Counting ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The color sensor does not independently command every turn.

Its information is used as part of the software's curve-validation system.

The current program groups the relevant readings into two navigation states:

```text
BLUE
```

and:

```text
ORANGE GROUP
```

where the software can interpret the required warm-color readings as the Orange track state.

A valid transition can be:

```text
BLUE → ORANGE
```

or:

```text
ORANGE → BLUE
```

The program also uses additional conditions such as steering confirmation and cooldown so that a single physical curve is not incorrectly counted multiple times.

For the complete Open Challenge:

```text
4 curves per lap
×
3 laps
=
12 validated curves
```

This makes the color sensor directly important to final parking because an incorrect curve count can make the parking procedure begin too early or too late.

---

# ❀ 2.3.12 HuskyLens Selection ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The Obstacle Challenge requires information that the ultrasonic and color sensors cannot provide.

The robot must distinguish between the competition's **red and green pillars**.

For this task, Cheese uses a **HuskyLens smart vision sensor**.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/sensor_placement_cam_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 HuskyLens camera placement"
       width="68%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Close-up of the current HuskyLens mounting position on Cheese v4.</em>
</p>

The HuskyLens performs visual recognition and provides information that can be transferred to the EV3 through the Arduino Nano communication bridge.

The current trained obstacle IDs are:

<div align="center">

| HuskyLens ID | Detected Pillar | Navigation Requirement |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **ID 1** | 🔴 Red | Pass on the **right** |
| **ID 2** | 🟢 Green | Pass on the **left** |

</div>

The camera therefore answers a different question from the ultrasonic sensors.

```text
ULTRASONIC
    ↓
HOW FAR?

HUSKYLENS
    ↓
WHAT COLOR / WHICH PILLAR?
```

# ❀ 2.3.13 HuskyLens Placement ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Camera placement is especially important because visual detection depends on the obstacle entering the camera's field of view.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/huskylens_mount_front_closeup_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 HuskyLens front mount"
       width="46%">
  &nbsp;
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/huskylens_angle_side_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 HuskyLens side viewing angle"
       width="46%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Front and side evidence of the current HuskyLens mounting position and viewing angle.</em>
</p>

A camera may be functioning correctly while still failing to detect an obstacle if:

- the obstacle is outside the field of view,
- Cheese exits a curve at a poor angle,
- detection occurs too late,
- another component blocks part of the view,
- or the robot is not positioned appropriately on the straight.

This became especially important during Obstacle Challenge testing.

A missed obstacle does not automatically mean:

```text
CAMERA FAILURE
```

It may instead mean:

```text
ROBOT POSITION
      ↓
POOR VIEWING ANGLE
      ↓
OBSTACLE OUTSIDE FOV
      ↓
NO USEFUL DETECTION
```

For this reason, camera placement, curve exit, obstacle approach, and software reaction must be considered as one connected system.

# ❀ 2.3.15 Arduino Nano and Sensor Communication ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The HuskyLens is not treated like a normal LEGO EV3 sensor.

Instead, Cheese uses an **Arduino Nano** as the communication bridge between the HuskyLens and the EV3.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/arduino_nano_position_v4.jpg"
       alt="Arduino Nano position on Cheese v4"
       width="62%">
</p>

<p align="center">
  <em>Current Arduino Nano position on Cheese v4.</em>
</p>

The communication architecture is:

```text
HUSKYLENS
    │
    │ I2C
    ▼
ARDUINO NANO
    │
    │ USB SERIAL
    ▼
EV3 BRICK
```

The Nano receives the camera information and sends a simplified result to the EV3.

This keeps the EV3 navigation code focused on deciding how the robot should move.

The current Nano position is intentionally practical rather than supported by a large dedicated tower.

During the v4 redesign, unnecessary mounting structure was removed while the Nano was kept in a safe position that still allows the required communication.

## ❀ 2.3.16 Sensor Placement and the v3 → v4 Redesign ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Sensor placement changed as part of the larger v4 simplification.

Cheese v3 contained more upper structure, camera support, auxiliary lighting hardware, and additional structural pieces.

Version 4 reduced this complexity.

Important sensing changes include:

- three ultrasonic sensors in the current navigation architecture,
- low lateral ultrasonic placement,
- a forward ultrasonic sensor,
- a more compact front sensing stack,
- a lower HuskyLens arrangement,
- a slightly lower color sensor,
- removal of the previous auxiliary lighting system,
- and simplified Nano placement.

These changes were not made only to reduce mass.

They also made the sensing system easier to understand physically.

# ❀ 2.3.17 Sensor Placement as an Engineering Decision ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

One of the main lessons from sensor development was:

> **Selecting the correct sensor is only half of the problem. The sensor must also be placed where it can observe the information the software expects.**

This relationship can be summarized as:

```text
SENSOR CAPABILITY
       +
PHYSICAL PLACEMENT
       +
CALIBRATION
       +
SOFTWARE INTERPRETATION
       ↓
USEFUL NAVIGATION INFORMATION
```

A problem at any one of these stages can appear as a navigation failure.

For example:

<div align="center">

| Observed Failure | Possible Sensor-Related Cause |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cheese drifts toward a wall | Lateral reading, filtering, calibration, or steering response |
| Color is missed | Sensor height, environmental conditions, or software validation |
| Front wall becomes too close | Forward measurement or recovery timing |
| Pillar is not detected | Camera FOV, robot angle, distance, or visibility |
| Correct pillar is detected but avoidance fails | Detection worked; movement response needs tuning |
| Next pillar is missed after avoidance | Recovery position may leave the camera facing poorly |

</div>

This distinction became especially important during testing because **not every apparent sensor failure is actually caused by the sensor**.

# ❀ 2.3.18 Current Sensor Roles: Open vs Obstacle ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The same physical robot uses its sensors differently depending on the challenge.

<div align="center">

| Sensor | Open Challenge | Obstacle Challenge |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Left Ultrasonic** | ✅ Wall/navigation information | ✅ Wall/navigation information |
| **Right Ultrasonic** | ✅ Wall/navigation information | ✅ Wall/navigation information |
| **Front Ultrasonic** | ✅ Safety/recovery | ✅ Forward safety support |
| **Color Sensor** | ✅ Curve counting | ✅ Track/curve information where used |
| **HuskyLens** | — Not required for Open navigation | ✅ Red/green pillar recognition |
| **Arduino Nano** | — Not required for Open navigation logic | ✅ Camera communication bridge |

</div>

This separation is important when debugging Cheese.

An Open Challenge problem should not automatically be blamed on the camera system because the Open navigation logic does not depend on HuskyLens obstacle recognition.

Likewise, successful Open navigation does not prove that the Obstacle Challenge vision and recovery system is calibrated.

# ❀ 2.3.20 Engineering Takeaway ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese v4's sensing architecture developed through repeated testing rather than from a single final design.

The team learned that sensor reliability depends on more than the electronic component itself.

It depends on:

- what the sensor is responsible for,
- where it is mounted,
- what direction it faces,
- its distance from the target,
- how the robot approaches that target,
- how readings are filtered,
- and how the software reacts to those readings.

The current architecture therefore gives each sensing system a defined role:

> **Ultrasonics measure space.  
> The Color Sensor measures track events.  
> HuskyLens identifies obstacles.  
> The Arduino Nano communicates vision data.  
> The EV3 combines that information into movement.**

This separation has made Cheese easier to test, debug, document, and continue improving.

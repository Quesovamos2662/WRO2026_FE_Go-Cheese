# ❀ 3.6.1 Tuning Philosophy ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Tuning Cheese was not simply changing values until the robot worked.

The team followed an engineering cycle:

```text
TEST
 ↓
OBSERVE FAILURE
 ↓
IDENTIFY CAUSE
 ↓
CHANGE ONE MAIN VARIABLE
 ↓
TEST AGAIN
 ↓
COMPARE
 ↓
KEEP / MODIFY / REVERSE
```

The main priority was:

```text
CONSISTENCY
    ↓
RELIABILITY
    ↓
SPEED
```

A faster robot is only useful if it can still complete the round reliably.

---

# ❀ 3.6.2 Real Tuning Order ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The order of tuning was important because later systems depended on earlier ones.

<div align="center">

| Order | Stage | Main Goal |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Mechanical | Make Cheese lighter and easier to control |
| **2** | Color Sensor | Improve blue/orange detection |
| **3** | PID | Achieve stable wall navigation |
| **4** | Corners | Improve turn shape and exits |
| **5** | Parking | Stop correctly after 12 curves |
| **6** | Obstacles | Improve detection, avoidance and recovery |

</div>

```text
MECHANICS
    ↓
SENSORS
    ↓
NAVIGATION
    ↓
CORNERS
    ↓
PARKING
    ↓
OBSTACLES
```

This prevented the team from trying to solve mechanical problems only through software.

---

# ❀ 3.6.3 Mechanical Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

One of the biggest improvements happened before software tuning.

Cheese v4 was reduced to **666 g**, compared with approximately **888.8 g** in v3.

Important mechanical changes included:

- reducing unnecessary structure,
- lowering and repositioning the EV3 Brick,
- changing the front wheel assemblies,
- simplifying the upper structure,
- removing the previous auxiliary lighting system,
- and creating a cleaner sensor arrangement.

<div align="center">

| Change | Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lower mass** | Easier acceleration, turning and recovery |
| **Improved EV3 position** | Better weight distribution |
| **New front wheels** | Better steering behavior |
| **Reduced upper structure** | Less unnecessary mass and complexity |
| **Cleaner sensor layout** | Easier calibration and maintenance |

</div>

The mechanical redesign made later PID and corner tuning more predictable.

---

# ❀ 3.6.4 Color Sensor Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Color detection was one of the early reliability problems.

Sunlight and sensor height affected readings during testing. In v4, the sensor remained in the same general structural area but was positioned slightly lower.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../v-photos/v4/color_sensor_floor_gap_v4.jpg"
       alt="Cheese v4 color sensor floor gap"
       width="58%">
</p>

This improved floor detection enough that external light-blocking structures are no longer part of normal v4 operation.

The final strategy combines:

```text
COLOR DETECTION
      +
BLUE ↔ ORANGE SEQUENCE
      +
STEERING CONFIRMATION
      +
COOLDOWN
      ↓
VALID CURVE
```

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../img/software-flowcharts%20v4/02_v4_color_detection_curve_counting.png"
       alt="Cheese v4 curve counting logic"
       width="82%">
</p>

Color tuning became important not only for lap counting, but also for activating parking at the correct point.

# ❀ 3.6.5 PID and Wall Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

PID became the base of Open navigation.

The goal was not to force Cheese into the exact center every second. Instead, the controller was tuned to make the minimum correction necessary while still protecting the robot from the walls.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../img/software-flowcharts%20v4/03_v4_minimum_correction_pid_controller.png"
       alt="Cheese v4 minimum correction PID"
       width="82%">
</p>

The tuning process moved from:

```text
TOO MUCH CORRECTION
      ↓
ZIG-ZAG
```

toward:

```text
SMALL ERROR
   ↓
SMALL CORRECTION

LARGE ERROR
   ↓
STRONGER CORRECTION
```

Progressive wall protection was added for situations where normal PID alone was not enough.

This became especially useful during close-wall situations and imperfect curve exits.

---

# ❀ 3.6.6 Corner Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Corner tuning focused on finding the balance between turns that were too wide and turns that were too aggressive.

```text
TOO WIDE
   ↓
OUTER WALL RISK

TOO SHARP
   ↓
INNER WALL RISK

CONTROLLED CURVE
   ↓
USEFUL EXIT POSITION
```

Testing showed that the exit was just as important as the turn itself.

For Open:

```text
BAD EXIT
   ↓
LARGE PID CORRECTION
   ↓
ZIG-ZAG / WALL RISK
```

For Obstacles:

```text
BAD EXIT
   ↓
BAD CAMERA HEADING
   ↓
PILLAR APPEARS LATE
   ↓
LATE AVOIDANCE
```

The complete corner-handling system is documented in **Section 3.5**.

---

# ❀ 3.6.7 Front-Wall Recovery Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The front ultrasonic provides an additional safety layer.

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../img/software-flowcharts%20v4/04_v4_front_wall_safety_reverse_recovery.png"
       alt="Cheese v4 front wall recovery"
       width="82%">
</p>

Instead of allowing Cheese to continue toward a dangerous front-wall condition:

```text
FRONT TOO CLOSE
      ↓
CONFIRM
      ↓
REVERSE
      ↓
CREATE SAFE SPACE
      ↓
RETURN TO NAVIGATION
```

The reverse behavior was tuned carefully because an overly aggressive recovery could prevent one collision but leave the robot badly positioned afterward.

# ❀ 3.6.8 Parking Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Parking could only be tuned properly after curve counting became reliable.

```text
12 CURVES COMPLETE
        ↓
PARKING ACTIVATES
        ↓
COMPARE FRONT DISTANCE
        ↓
SLOW APPROACH
        ↓
FINAL CREEP
        ↓
STOP
```

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../img/software-flowcharts%20v4/06_v4_parking_finish_logic.png"
       alt="Cheese v4 parking logic"
       width="82%">
</p>

The current system uses the starting front-distance reference instead of depending only on elapsed time.

Current observations:

- Cheese stops inside the parking area.
- Overshooting is not a main current problem.
- Stopping too short is not a main current problem.
- The final position can sometimes be slightly angled.
- Incorrect curve counting can still affect parking timing.

Parking is therefore functional but still receives final adjustments.

# ❀ 3.6.9 Obstacle Tuning ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Obstacle tuning remains the most experimental part of Cheese v4.

The current vision architecture is:

```text
HUSKYLENS
    ↓
ARDUINO NANO
    ↓
EV3
    ↓
CAMERA BIAS
    ↓
STEERING RESPONSE
```

<p align="center">
  <img src="../../img/software-flowcharts%20v4/05_v4_obstacle_detection_camera_bias.png"
       alt="Cheese v4 obstacle detection and camera bias"
       width="82%">
</p>

The current trained pillar mapping is:

<div align="center">

| Pillar | HuskyLens | Required Passing Side |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| 🟢 **Green** | **Color ID 1** | **Left** |
| 🔴 **Red** | **Color ID 2** | **Right** |

</div>

The most important obstacle tuning variables have been:

- camera position,
- camera viewing angle,
- driving speed,
- detection timing,
- avoidance strength,
- interaction between camera bias and PID,
- and recovery after passing a pillar.

The current vision system can successfully recognize both competition pillar colors.

<div align="center">

| Pillar | HuskyLens Training | Required Passing Side |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| 🟢 **Green** | **Color ID 1** | **Left** |
| 🔴 **Red** | **Color ID 2** | **Right** |

Detection itself is already functional. The larger tuning challenge is converting a correct visual detection into a reliable physical maneuver.

<p align="center">
  <strong>
    Detect Pillar → React Early → Pass Correct Side → Clear Pillar → Recover → Continue
  </strong>
</p>

A correct camera classification alone does not guarantee a successful obstacle pass. Cheese must detect the pillar early enough, apply sufficient steering, clear the obstacle without carrying it, and then return to stable navigation.

This distinction became important during testing:

<p align="center">
  <strong>
    Correct Detection ≠ Successful Avoidance
  </strong>
</p>

For this reason, current obstacle tuning focuses not only on HuskyLens recognition, but also on **reaction timing, avoidance strength, pillar clearance, and post-obstacle recovery**.

---


# ❀ 3.6.11 Biggest Current Obstacle Problem: Recovery ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The most important remaining obstacle problem is what happens **after** Cheese avoids a pillar.

A successful avoidance can still become a failed run if Cheese does not recover correctly.

```text
PILLAR AVOIDED
      ↓
BAD EXIT HEADING
      ↓
ROBOT REMAINS OFFSET
      ↓
CAMERA VIEW CHANGES
      ↓
NEXT EVENT BECOMES HARDER
```

Current tuning therefore focuses heavily on:

```text
AVOID
  ↓
CLEAR
  ↓
RETURN TO PID
  ↓
RECENTER
  ↓
NEXT PILLAR
```

This is currently more important than simply making avoidance stronger.

---

# ❀ 3.6.12 Failure → Cause → Change → Result ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The following table summarizes some of the most important tuning decisions.

<div align="center">

| Failure | Cause | Change | Result / Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Robot difficult to control** | Excess structure and mass | Reduced v4 mass | More predictable movement |
| **Color readings inconsistent** | Sensor height / lighting | Lowered sensor + improved logic | Better curve detection |
| **Robot zig-zagged** | Excessive correction | Minimum-correction PID | Smoother navigation |
| **Wall crashes** | PID insufficient near danger | Progressive wall protection | Fewer wall contacts |
| **Front collision risk** | Dangerous forward approach | Reverse recovery | Creates safe space |
| **Double curve count** | Repeated color event | Sequence + cooldown | One count per curve |
| **Wide curves** | Steering too weak / late | Curve tuning | Better exit position |
| **Parking inconsistency** | Finish depended on progress errors | Saved distance reference | Stop inside area |
| **Pillar detected late** | Camera angle / speed | Camera adjustment + lower speed | Earlier reaction |
| **Pillar carried** | Late / weak avoidance | Tune timing and camera bias | Clear pillar safely |
| **Next obstacle fails** | Poor recovery | Improve return to PID | Stable next approach |

</div>

This process helped the team identify the real source of each problem instead of changing unrelated values.

# ❀ 3.6.13 v3 → v4 Tuning Progress ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese v4 was developed directly from problems observed in earlier versions.

<div align="center">

| Earlier Problem | v4 Response |
| :--- | :--- |
| Heavy structure | Reduced robot to **666 g** |
| Less favorable weight distribution | Repositioned EV3 |
| Steering needed improvement | Changed front wheel configuration |
| Color affected by lighting | Lower sensor position + improved logic |
| Wide / unstable curves | PID + curve tuning |
| Front-wall crashes | Ultrasonic reverse recovery |
| Parking inconsistency | Curve count + saved distance reference |
| Obstacle reaction problems | Camera, speed and avoidance tuning |
| Extra lighting complexity | Auxiliary lights removed |

</div>

This makes v4 an engineering response to previous testing rather than simply a visual redesign.

# ❀ 3.6.15 Remaining Tuning Priorities ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

### Open Challenge

```text
CURVE COUNT RELIABILITY
        ↓
CURVE CONSISTENCY
        ↓
PARKING ALIGNMENT
```

### Obstacle Challenge

```text
EARLIER DETECTION
      ↓
RELIABLE AVOIDANCE
      ↓
CLEAN PILLAR CLEARANCE
      ↓
FAST RECOVERY
      ↓
NEXT PILLAR
      ↓
OBSTACLE PARKING
```

Battery condition also remains important because weaker motor response can change steering and timing during otherwise similar tests.

# ✦ Engineering Lessons ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ───

### 1. Mechanics should be tuned before software

A lighter and better-balanced robot made later control adjustments easier.

### 2. Change one important variable at a time

Changing many values simultaneously makes it difficult to identify what actually improved the robot.

### 3. A successful subsystem does not guarantee a successful run

Correct color detection does not guarantee correct parking, and correct pillar detection does not guarantee successful avoidance.

### 4. Recovery matters as much as avoidance

Passing one obstacle is not enough if Cheese becomes badly positioned for the next one.

### 5. Speed must follow reliability

Performance was increased only when the robot remained controllable.

### 6. Failures are useful engineering data

Wall collisions, missed lines, wide corners, carried pillars, and bad recoveries all helped identify the next tuning priority.

# ✦ 3.6 Tuning Process Summary ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ───

Cheese v4 was developed through an iterative engineering process rather than a single final design.

The tuning sequence was:

```text
MECHANICS
    ↓
COLOR SENSOR
    ↓
PID + WALL CONTROL
    ↓
CORNERS
    ↓
PARKING
    ↓
OBSTACLE DETECTION
    ↓
AVOIDANCE
    ↓
RECOVERY
```

The main development principle was:

```text
FAILURE
   ↓
UNDERSTAND WHY
   ↓
CONTROLLED CHANGE
   ↓
TEST
   ↓
MEASURE IMPROVEMENT
```

The **Open Challenge is close to final**, while the **Obstacle Challenge remains under active tuning**, especially for reliable recovery after pillar avoidance.

<p align="center">
  <strong>
    Test → Observe → Understand → Adjust → Retest → Improve
  </strong>
</p>

## ❀ 1.3.1 Steering & Drive Architecture ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese separates propulsion and steering into two mechanical functions.

| System | Main Component | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Drive** | EV3 Large Motor | Provides propulsion through the rear wheels. |
| **Steering** | EV3 Medium Motor | Changes the angle of the front wheels. |
| **Steering linkage** | Technic axles, pins, connectors and liftarms | Transfers motor rotation to the front wheels. |
| **Front geometry** | Ackermann steering assembly | Allows the front wheels to follow different turning radii. |
| **Chassis** | LEGO Technic structure | Keeps the motors, axles and steering mechanism aligned. |

The two systems must work together. Driving speed determines how quickly Cheese reaches a corner, while steering response determines whether the robot can establish the required wheel angle before reaching the wall.

## ❀ 1.3.3 Front Steering System ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The **EV3 Medium Motor** controls steering.

Unlike the Large Motor, it does not propel Cheese forward. Its rotation is transferred through the steering linkage to change the orientation of the front wheels.

| Steering Part | Function | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EV3 Medium Motor** | Generates steering movement. | Controls wheel direction. |
| **Linkage** | Transfers motor motion. | Converts rotation into wheel-angle change. |
| **Front wheels** | Establish the direction of travel. | Determine the robot's turning path. |
| **Axles and pivots** | Allow controlled movement. | Keep the mechanism movable. |
| **Structural supports** | Hold the system aligned. | Reduce unwanted movement or flex. |

## ❀ 1.3.4 Steering Linkage ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The Medium Motor does not directly rotate the front wheels.

Instead, motor movement passes through a mechanical linkage made from LEGO Technic elements.

| Step | Mechanical Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Medium Motor rotates. |
| **2** | The linkage transfers the movement. |
| **3** | Steering arms change position. |
| **4** | Front-wheel angles change. |
| **5** | Cheese follows the resulting curved path. |

## ❀ 1.3.5 Ackermann Steering Geometry ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Cheese uses **Ackermann steering geometry**.

During a turn, the inner and outer front wheels travel around different circles. The inner wheel follows a smaller radius, while the outer wheel follows a larger radius.

Therefore:

<p align="center">
  <strong>θ inner &gt; θ outer</strong>
</p>

The inner wheel must turn more sharply than the outer wheel.

### Mathematical model

Let:

| Symbol | Meaning |
| :---: | :--- |
| **R** | Turning radius measured from the turning center |
| **T** | Front track width |
| **L** | Wheelbase |
| **θ inner** | Inner-wheel steering angle |
| **θ outer** | Outer-wheel steering angle |

The two wheel paths can be represented as:

<p align="center">
  <strong>Inner radius = R − T/2</strong><br>
  <strong>Outer radius = R + T/2</strong>
</p>

and:

<p align="center">
  <strong>tan(θ inner) = L / (R − T/2)</strong><br>
  <strong>tan(θ outer) = L / (R + T/2)</strong>
</p>

Because the inner radius is smaller:

<p align="center">
  <strong>θ inner &gt; θ outer</strong>
</p>

This geometry reduces the need for the front wheels to slide sideways through a curve.

### Why this matters

If both wheels were forced to follow an inappropriate identical steering path, additional tire scrub could occur.

| Effect | Consequence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tire scrub** | Additional friction during turns |
| **Side loading** | Extra force on steering components |
| **Poor repeatability** | Corners may behave differently |
| **Energy loss** | Motor effort is wasted through friction |

## ❀ 1.3.6 Steering Development: Failure → Fix ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

The current steering mechanism was not created in one attempt.

Testing across the different versions exposed mechanical problems that changed the design.

| Version | Observation | Engineering Response | Lesson |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **v1** | Original linkage was too stiff. | Changed the joint configuration. | A steering linkage must pivot freely. |
| **v2** | Revised joint moved more freely. | Working geometry was retained. | Lower mechanical resistance improved steering movement. |
| **v3** | Aggressive turns stressed the front structure. | Steering support was reinforced. | A good linkage still requires a rigid foundation. |
| **v4** | Excess structure added unnecessary mass and complexity. | Surrounding structure was simplified. | Reinforcement should exist only where mechanically necessary. |

## ❀ 1.3.8 Why the v4 Mechanical Redesign Matters ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

One of the largest changes between v3 and v4 was the reduction of unnecessary structure.

<p align="center">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/V3-888_g-FF8FAB?style=for-the-badge">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/Mass_Removed-222_g-FFD43B?style=for-the-badge">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/V4-666_g-57C785?style=for-the-badge">
</p>

The robot mass decreased from approximately **888 g to 666 g**, a reduction of **222 g**.

The goal was not to remove structural support randomly. Critical steering and motor supports were retained while unnecessary upper structure was removed.

| v4 Change | Engineering Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **222 g mass reduction** | Less total mass must change direction during a turn. |
| **Lower EV3 position** | Produces a lower and cleaner central structure. |
| **Horizontal Large Motor placement** | Uses chassis volume more efficiently. |
| **Reduced upper structure** | Removes components that do not directly support steering. |
| **Shorter cable routing** | Reduces clutter around mechanical systems. |
| **Critical supports retained** | Preserves steering alignment. |

## ❀ 1.3.9 Steering Stability During Curves ────୨ৎ────────୨ৎ────

Mechanical steering and software control directly affect each other.

A mechanically correct steering mechanism can still produce a poor corner if the robot approaches too quickly. Likewise, good software cannot compensate indefinitely for a loose or restricted mechanical linkage.

| Failure | Possible Cause | Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Late turn** | Robot reaches the corner before sufficient steering angle develops. | Adjust speed and curve timing. |
| **Turn too sharp** | Steering correction is excessive. | Reduce or smooth steering command. |
| **Linkage stress** | Sudden steering movement loads the structure. | Maintain strong supports and smoother control. |
| **Poor curve exit** | Steering does not return appropriately after the corner. | Tune return-to-center behavior. |
| **Wall contact** | Speed, steering angle and timing are not synchronized. | Tune the complete motion system together. |

This became one of the main lessons from testing:

<p align="center">
  <strong>Driving speed must match steering response.</strong>
</p>

## ✦ 1.3 Engineering Conclusions ─── ⋆⋅☆⋅⋆ ───

The development of Cheese showed that steering performance cannot be attributed to one component.

The **EV3 Large Motor** provides propulsion, the **EV3 Medium Motor** controls the steering linkage, and the **Ackermann geometry** determines how the front wheels follow a curve. Their performance also depends on chassis rigidity, robot mass, motor mounting, linkage freedom, speed and software tuning.

The progression from v1 through v4 followed a repeated engineering process:

<p align="center">
  <strong>
    Failure → Observation → Mechanical Change → Testing → Further Refinement
  </strong>
</p>

The current v4 design keeps the important steering geometry and structural supports while removing unnecessary mass and upper structure. This produced a **666 g** robot with a cleaner and more accessible steering and drivetrain arrangement.

<p align="center">
  <strong>Key lesson:</strong><br>
  The lightest steering system is not automatically the best one.
  The goal is to remove unnecessary structure while preserving the rigidity,
  alignment and freedom of movement required for repeatable steering.
</p>
